Your Mission Journey

Complete a mission at your own pace. Always ask permission before writing, recording, or sharing someone’s story.

0 of 6 missions complete
MISSION 1

Memory Keeper

Invite an elder, family member, or trusted community member to share a favorite childhood memory.

Try asking:

“What is one memory from your childhood that still makes you smile?”

“Who was there with you?”

“What would you like younger generations to understand about that time?”

MISSION 2

Tradition Detective

Learn about a family or community tradition, celebration, craft, song, recipe, or way of helping others.

Try asking:

“How did this tradition begin?”

“Who taught you?”

“How has it changed over time?”

MISSION 3

Wisdom for Tomorrow

Ask someone older what they hope the next generation will remember, protect, or carry forward.

Try asking:

“What lesson has life taught you that you hope young people will carry forward?”

“What are you proud to pass on?”

MISSION 4

Skill Share

Invite someone to explain or demonstrate a skill they learned through life, work, family, or community.

Try asking:

“Who taught you this skill?”

“What do beginners often get wrong?”

“Would you like this skill to be passed on?”

MISSION 5

Objects with Stories

Ask someone about an object that matters to them, such as a photograph, tool, keepsake, recipe card, or handmade item. They can choose not to show it.

Try asking:

“What makes this object meaningful to you?”

“How did it come into your life?”

“What would you want someone younger to know about it?”

MISSION 6

Community Helpers

Learn about a way someone has helped their family or community. The story may be about volunteering, caring for others, solving a problem, or working together.

Try asking:

“What made you want to help?”

“Who worked alongside you?”

“What did that experience teach you?”

Facilitation Resources

Before the session

  • Explain the purpose, choices, and boundaries before asking anyone to share.
  • Ask storytellers what may be written down, recorded, or shared—and honor a no.
  • Offer alternatives: speak, draw, write, listen, or pass.

During the session

  • Ground: Build a welcoming space and remind everyone they control their story.
  • Guide: Use open questions; don't pressure anyone to answer.
  • Witness: Listen without interrupting, correcting, or rushing.
  • Affirm: Thank the storyteller without judging or interpreting their experience for them.
  • Archive: Confirm permission before saving or sharing anything.

Helpful phrases

“You can skip any question.”

“Would you like me to write that down, or would you prefer I just listen?”

“Is there any part of this story you would like kept private?”

“Thank you for trusting us with that memory.”

If a story brings up strong feelings

Pause, offer choice and privacy, and ask what support the person wants. Don't press for details or promise secrecy if a safety concern requires following your organization's safeguarding procedures.

Discussion Starters

What can we learn from the people who came before us?

Which traditions or skills do you hope future generations will remember?

What helps a person feel safe and respected when sharing a memory?
